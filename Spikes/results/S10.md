## S10: a live Metal render loop inside the render host

Run 2026-09-23, macOS 27.0, Apple M5 (10-core GPU), built-in display at 1800×1169 @2x (a 3600×2338
backing store), 120 Hz. The change is `Spikes/S10/metal-in-extension.patch`. It compiles only with
`SWIFT_ACTIVE_COMPILATION_CONDITIONS='$(inherited) LIVEPAPER_S10'` and is driven at run time by the Darwin
notification `app.livepaper.s10` (state 0, the default, is the product's behaviour). The user asked to
stop early, so the energy cases, cover and sleep were not run; this record covers only what the spike
found about Metal in the extension. The log lines are in `raw/s10-in-extension.log`.

### What was built

- **The layer.** A `CAMetalLayer` (`bgra8Unorm`, framebuffer-only, opaque, three drawables, display
  sync on) goes into `SurfaceLayers.root` in `HostedSurfaces.host`, *before* `context.layer = layers.root`,
  at opacity 0, and is laid out to the surface (drawable 3600×2338). Showing it later is an opacity
  change, never a new layer.
- **The supervisor drives it.** A stand-in `SurfacePlayback` wraps `SurfaceLayers`. On play, the layers
  hold the poster (with the decoder released), the Metal layer is shown and the loop runs. On pause and
  suspend, the loop stops (a suspend also frees the heavy load's textures). On hold-still or nothing, the
  Metal layer is hidden. The watchdog gets no count for the Metal picture.
- **Loads.** The light load is one full-screen pass: a textured quad with a water-ripple fragment shader.
  The heavy load is 20 full-resolution passes: the scene, five separable 13-tap Gaussian pairs, 64-sample
  radial god rays, a threshold, two bloom blur pairs, combine, chromatic aberration, and a grade into the
  drawable.
- **The link.** `CAMetalDisplayLink` runs on a thread with its own run loop, with
  `preferredFrameRateRange` pinned to the target. The `CVDisplayLink` and timer fallbacks are in the
  patch but were not run, because the display link worked.
- **What is measured.** Pacing comes from each drawable's presented time (`addPresentedHandler`),
  lateness from the link's `targetPresentationTimestamp`, and GPU time from the command buffer's
  `gpuStartTime`/`gpuEndTime`. It is logged every 5 s as `s10: stats …`.

### Results

- **It composites (measured).** The capture of the desktop's wallpaper window (WindowManager's, by
  window id) showed the shader, with 18.8 % of its pixels changing in 0.5 s. 13,483 of 13,496 light-60
  frames reported a presented time. The 13 that did not came at loop starts (inferred: before the opacity
  change reached the render server). So Phosphene's rule holds and is enough: the layer has to be in the
  tree before hosting, and it can be shown later.
- **The link fires in the extension (measured).** The first callback came 5 to 20 ms after each start,
  and the first presented frame 55 to 115 ms after. The layer sits in a remote context with no window,
  and the link needed none.
- **Stops and restarts on mode changes (measured).** Each stop invalidates the link, and each restart
  had its first callback within 20 ms. Cover, uncover, sleep and wake were **not** run.

- **Light at 60 and 30, and heavy at 30: steady (measured).** In every window the 95th percentile of the
  presented interval equals the frame duration, and lateness against the link's target is about 0. Light
  at 30 had one 133 ms hitch while another agent's ffmpeg encodes were running.
- **Heavy at 60 does not keep up here (measured).** It takes 34 to 45 ms of GPU a frame, and frames came
  every 16.7 or 25 ms (a mean of 22 ms, about 45 fps), up to 58 ms late.
- **The GPU numbers come from a busy machine.** Most windows ran during a FaceTime call, which held the
  GPU at about 72 % before the spike drew anything, with the user active in other apps. GPU time from
  the command buffer includes time spent sharing the GPU, so an idle Mac should do better (inferred).
  Light frames took longer at 30 fps than at 60 (inferred: the GPU clocks down under less load).

### For whoever measures energy next

- **top's power score does not see the GPU (observed, not measured).** In the runs that were taken
  (every one of them had other load and was discarded), the extension's score tracked its CPU: 3.3 for
  heavy at 60 with the GPU at 99–100 %, below light at 60's 4.2. S2's budget is written in those scores,
  so it cannot see the main cost of a Metal loop. It needs the GPU's "Device Utilization %" from
  `ioreg -c IOAccelerator` and, for watts, `powermetrics`.
- **Measure on a quiet machine.** Other agents' ffmpeg encodes discarded most runs, and the user's
  windows covered 90 % of the desktop, which hides most of WindowServer's compositing cost.
  `Spikes/S10/energy.py`, `timeline.py`, `cover.swift` (a covering window ordered behind every other
  window) and `coverage.swift` are ready to use. Their paths point at this session's scratch folder.
- **Swapping builds.** Replacing `build/Frozen/Livepaper.app` at the same path did not kill the extension
  instance that was already running (the hazard in record 0001 did not fire). The relaunched app then
  took that old instance's heartbeat as live. Ending that process let the app's ladder restart the agent,
  50 s later, onto the new build.
