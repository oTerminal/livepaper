### What the agent does with Spaces

One surface per display, not per Space. A new Space or a fullscreen Space gets its own "Wallpaper" window, and
the agent shows the surface it already has in it: no `ACQUIRE`, no `INVALIDATE`, no `UPDATE` for any of the steps
above. So the key for "which video" is the display, as the roadmap assumed, and Spaces cost nothing.

### Covered means not composited

With a fullscreen app over a display, `displayedPixelBuffer()` stops changing on that display's surface (0 in
2 s) although the engine keeps enqueueing. Two consequences for M5: the "is playback advancing" check must not
call a covered display a failure, and the same signal is a free way to know a display is fully covered
(a candidate for pausing decode there). A capture of the covered window still shows the picture moving, so
nothing is wrong with the surface.

### Resolution: the agent stretches, it does not ask again

A mode change on the external sends the extension nothing. The Wallpaper window takes the new size and the
surface made for 1920x1080 is scaled into it, each axis on its own: at 1280x1024 all six colour bars are still
there, each 1/6 of the width, where an aspect fill would have cut the outer two down to slivers. Same aspect
ratio (1280x720): looks right. M5 has to watch `NSApplication.didChangeScreenParametersNotification` (or the
CoreGraphics reconfiguration callback) itself and re-lay the surface's layers for the display's new aspect
ratio. Whether the agent then stretches the corrected layer again is not tried.

### Unplug and replug, lock with two displays

Done by Vaibhav the same evening, eyes on; the times and IDs are from the extension's log.

| Step | Seen | Log |
|---|---|---|
| Cable out for about 6 s | The monitor came back on clip B, moving | `INVALIDATE` 19:43:49, `ACQUIRE` 19:43:55: display 3, UUID `1ED56131-…` again, on clip B 15 ms later |
| Cable out for about 97 s, past the 15 s teardown | The same | Surface torn down 19:44:30, `ACQUIRE` 19:45:52: display 3, the same UUID, on clip B 10 ms later |
| Lock with both attached | Video on both lock screens, no grey going in or coming out | `presentationMode=locked` then `default`; 60 new pictures in 2 s on both displays after unlock |

The display UUID and the CoreGraphics display ID both survived the replug, so the per-display choice can be
keyed by UUID as planned. The agent never re-acquires the old surface: every replug is a new surface ID, and the
old one goes at its 15 s timeout (or sooner than the replug, in the long case). So the 15 s grace buys nothing
for displays, and the video for a returning display has to come from the UUID, not from anything remembered
per surface. The replug also settles the first row of the table: plugging in is enough, the agent acquires a
surface without anyone opening Settings. A monitor this Mac has never seen is the one case not tried.
